## What This Does

TakeMeter is a text classifier that will categorize discussion comments from Hacker News, a public online technology community. I examined comments from three discussions covering JavaScript runtimes, technology hiring practices, and AI decision models.

From these discussions, I identified three candidate distinctions: technical or factual explanations, personal experiences, and unsupported opinions. These distinctions will guide the label taxonomy developed in the next milestone.





---

## Label Taxonomy

<!-- Each label: a one-sentence definition and two real examples from your
     reading. Then your decision rule for the hardest boundary.

     The decision rule is worth a point on its own and it's the thing most
     people leave out. Every taxonomy has a hardest boundary. Name yours. -->

### `technical_explanation`

**Definition:** A comment that primarily explains a technical concept, factual detail, or how something works.

**Example 1:** `flohofwoe` explains that Deno includes a built-in TypeScript type checker, linter, formatter, and test runner, whereas Node relies on separate tools.

**Example 2:** `bayesianbot` clarifies that version 3.26.0 refers to the OpenAI Python SDK rather than the Python programming language.

### `personal_experience`

**Definition:** A comment that primarily recounts something the commenter personally did, witnessed, or experienced.

**Example 1:** `cscheid` describes leading a project built on Deno and encountering compatibility and tooling problems.

**Example 2:** `bkolobara` describes considering Oxide's interview process before accepting an offer from another company.

### `unsupported_opinion`

**Definition:** A comment that primarily expresses a judgment or assertion without providing specific supporting evidence.

**Example 1:** `redox99` claims that migrating away from Deno can be completed in a single day.

**Example 2:** `Oras` questions what Jev could meaningfully scale after competing implementations appeared.

### The hardest boundary

**Which two labels:** `technical_explanation` and `unsupported_opinion`

**The decision rule I used every time:** If a comment makes an assertion and provides specific, checkable supporting details, classify it as `technical_explanation`. If it makes an assertion without such details, classify it as `unsupported_opinion`. If the comment primarily recounts something the author personally experienced, classify it as `personal_experience` instead.

## The Dataset

<!-- Where you collected from, how you labelled, your counts, and three hard
     cases. -->

**Where the posts came from:**

I collected 200 public comments from Hacker News using its official API. The comments came from discussions about Dropbox, Cloudflare's acquisition of Deno, Oxide Computer's Series D funding, and Typesafe AI's fundraising.

**How I labelled them:**

I used the three-label taxonomy defined above to categorize the comments according to their primary purpose. AI was used to suggest classifications, with the taxonomy and decision rule serving as the reference for resolving ambiguous cases. The dataset was checked for missing text, invalid labels, duplicate comments, and label distribution.

**Counts per label:**

| Label | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `technical_explanation` | 57 | 28.5% |
| `personal_experience` | 31 | 15.5% |
| `unsupported_opinion` | 112 | 56.0% |
| **Total** | **200** | **100%** |

**Three hard cases**

**1. Technical explanation versus unsupported opinion**

> **The post:** A commenter explained that Linux users could combine FTP mounting with SVN or CVS to create an alternative to Dropbox.
>
> **Could have been:** `technical_explanation` or `unsupported_opinion`
>
> **I chose:** `technical_explanation`, because the comment identifies specific technologies and explains how they could be combined to accomplish a task, even though it also criticizes Dropbox.

**2. Personal experience versus unsupported opinion**

> **The post:** A commenter described considering Dropbox for backing up code before questioning whether they could trust an unfamiliar developer with their files.
>
> **Could have been:** `personal_experience` or `unsupported_opinion`
>
> **I chose:** `personal_experience`, because the comment primarily describes the author's thought process and personal reaction to the application.

**3. Technical explanation versus unsupported opinion**

> **The post:** A commenter suggested that Dropbox should support offline synchronization because users sometimes lack internet access.
>
> **Could have been:** `technical_explanation` or `unsupported_opinion`
>
> **I chose:** `technical_explanation`, because the recommendation is supported by a specific technical limitation of online-only file access.
